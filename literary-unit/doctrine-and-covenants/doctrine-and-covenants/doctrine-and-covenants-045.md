## Genre

D&C 45 is a **revelation in the voice of the Lord**: direct, first-person divine speech addressed to the Church. Within that frame it combines four forms:

- **Prophetic exhortation:** repeated calls to "hearken"
- **Intercessory speech:** Christ's plea to the Father (vv. 3–5)
- **Apocalyptic prophecy:** signs of the last days (vv. 16–59)
- **Practical commandment:** translation, gathering, and secrecy (vv. 60–75)

## A Speech Within a Speech

The section's most distinctive feature is its **frame structure**:

1. **Present-day address (vv. 1–15):** Christ speaks to the Church of 1831.
2. **Embedded retrospective discourse (vv. 16–59):** Christ quotes what he said to his ancient disciples on the Mount of Olives ("I will show it plainly as I showed it unto my disciples," v. 16). Even the disciples' reaction is reported (v. 34).
3. **Present-day application (vv. 60–75):** The voice returns to 1831 with direct instructions.

The frame lets the revelation present the ancient prophecy as newly restored and plain. It ties the disciples' anxious questions in Jerusalem to the Saints' questions in Kirtland.

## The "Hearken" Refrain

The opening is structured by repeated summons: "Hearken" (v. 1), "hearken unto my voice" (v. 2), "Listen to him" (v. 3), "Hearken… listen together" (v. 6), "hearken ye together" (v. 11), and "hearken and I will reason with you" (v. 15). Each summons introduces a new portrait of Christ: **Creator** (v. 1), **Advocate** (vv. 3–5), **Light and Covenant-giver** (vv. 7–10), and **God of Enoch** (vv. 11–14). The phrase "reason… as with men in days of old" (vv. 10, 15) bridges the introduction into the prophecy.

## Christ's Plea (vv. 3–5)

This short passage is a **prayer embedded in a revelation**. Christ addresses the Father in direct speech with a three-part appeal: *behold* the sufferings, *behold* the blood, *spare* these my brethren. Its legal imagery of an advocate "pleading your cause" frames the whole section: the coming judgment is announced by the one who intercedes for those facing it.

## Intertextual Weaving

D&C 45 is densely built from biblical language, recombined and expanded:

- **John's prologue** (vv. 7–8): Alpha and Omega, the light shining in darkness, coming to his own, power to become sons of God (John 1:5, 11–12).
- **Jeremiah 8:20** (v. 2): the summer past and the harvest ended.
- **Psalm 95 / Hebrews 3** (v. 6): "while it is called today… harden not your hearts."
- **Hebrews 11:13–16** (vv. 12–14): strangers and pilgrims seeking a city.
- **Matthew 24 / Luke 21** (vv. 16–44): temple destruction, wars and rumors of wars, love waxing cold, the fig tree, the times of the Gentiles.
- **Joel 2:30–31** (vv. 41–42): blood, fire, vapors of smoke; the sun darkened and the moon turned to blood.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:16–17** (v. 45): the trump sounds and the saints rise to meet the Lord.
- **Zechariah 14:4; 12:10; 13:6** (vv. 48–53): the Mount of Olives splitting; the Jews looking on the pierced one; wounds received "in the house of my friends."
- **Matthew 25:1–13** (vv. 56–57): the ten virgins, reinterpreted as those who take the Holy Spirit for their guide.

## Motifs That Bind the Section

- **Light:** Christ is the light that darkness does not comprehend (v. 7). The covenant is sent as a light (v. 9), and in the last days "a light shall break forth" (vv. 28, 36). Those who reject it "perceive not the light" (v. 29).
- **Summer and harvest:** "the summer shall be past" (v. 2) is answered by the fig tree's leaves showing "summer is now nigh" (v. 37). The same seasonal image moves from warning to hope.
- **City:** Enoch's city reserved in heaven (v. 12) and the New Jerusalem on earth (vv. 66–71) bracket the prophecy. The city sought by "holy men" of old becomes the Saints' own task.
- **Fear:** "Be not troubled" (v. 35) for the disciples, versus the "terror of the Lord" for the wicked (vv. 67, 74–75). Zion is "terrible" to its enemies (v. 70) but a place of safety for the Saints (v. 66).

## Climactic Recognition Scene (vv. 51–53)

The prophecy reaches its emotional peak in a short **dialogue**. The Jews ask about the wounds, and Christ answers with three escalating "I am" declarations: he who was *lifted up*, *Jesus that was crucified*, and *the Son of God* (v. 52). Their weeping follows, and the scene turns from cosmic catastrophe to personal reconciliation.

## Closing

The section ends where it began, with the Lord's power over all nations, and closes with the formal **"Even so. Amen."** It moves from creation (v. 1) to consummation (v. 75), and from the Lord as advocate to the Lord as judge.
