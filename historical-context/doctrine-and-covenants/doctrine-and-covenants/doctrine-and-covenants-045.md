## Date, Place, and Recipients

Joseph Smith dictated this revelation at **Kirtland, Ohio, on March 7, 1831**. It is addressed broadly to "ye people of my church" (vv. 1, 6) rather than to a single person. The Church was not yet a year old (organized April 6, 1830).

## The Situation in Early 1831

- **The move to Ohio:** In December 1830 and January 1831, revelations directed the New York Saints to gather to Ohio (D&C 37; 38:31–32). Joseph Smith arrived in Kirtland around **February 1, 1831**, and New York members followed through the spring. Kirtland already had a large group of converts from the mission of Oliver Cowdery, Parley P. Pratt, and others, who had passed through on their way to preach to Native Americans on the Missouri frontier.
- **Hostile reports:** Joseph Smith's history introduces this revelation by noting that many false reports and stories about the Church were being published and circulated to discourage people from investigating it. The revelation came, the history says, to the joy of the Saints. Its broad view of God's plan for the last days answered a time of ridicule and uncertainty.
- **The law and Zion:** A month earlier, D&C 42 ("the law") had promised that the location of the **New Jerusalem** would be revealed (D&C 42:9, 62). Members were eager to know when and where Zion would be built.
- **A millennial age:** Many American Christians in the early 1830s expected the Second Coming soon and read current events as signs of the end. D&C 45 addresses those expectations in Restoration terms.

## Connection to Joseph Smith's Bible Translation

This revelation is closely tied to Joseph Smith's **inspired revision of the Bible** (the Joseph Smith Translation, or JST).

- **Enoch (vv. 11–14):** Joseph had recently received the extended account of **Enoch and his city, Zion**, now Moses 7 (December 1830), which explains this revelation's reference to Enoch's city "reserved until a day of righteousness."
- **The New Testament (vv. 60–61):** The Lord tells Joseph that he may now begin translating the **New Testament**, where these things "shall be made known." Joseph began the New Testament the very next day, **March 8, 1831**, pausing his work on Genesis.
- **Matthew 24:** The Olivet Discourse retold in vv. 16–59 parallels Joseph's later revision of Matthew 24, published in the Pearl of Great Price as **Joseph Smith—Matthew**. Reading the two together is one of the best ways to study this section.

## The Olivet Discourse Background

In vv. 16–59 the Lord recounts what he taught his disciples on the Mount of Olives shortly before his crucifixion (Matthew 24; Mark 13; Luke 21). That discourse addressed two questions: when the temple would be destroyed (fulfilled when Rome destroyed Jerusalem in **AD 70**) and what signs would precede his Second Coming. D&C 45 keeps the two questions distinct and adds Restoration details: the **"times of the Gentiles"** (Luke 21:24) climax in a light that breaks forth as "the fulness of my gospel" (v. 28). It also includes material from Zechariah's prophecies of the Mount of Olives splitting (Zech. 14:4) and of the Jews recognizing the wounded Messiah (Zech. 12:10; 13:6).

## Zion and the Westward Gathering (vv. 64–71)

The command to "gather ye out from the eastern lands" and go "into the western countries" fit the Church's movement from New York to Ohio and anticipated the next step. The promised **New Jerusalem**, a city of refuge called Zion, echoes the Book of Mormon (Ether 13:2–10; 3 Nephi 20:22; 21:23–24) and Moses 7:62. Four months later, in **July 1831**, a revelation identified **Independence, Jackson County, Missouri**, as the center place of Zion (D&C 57).

## Reading the Prophecies in Their Time

- **"Wars in your own lands" (v. 63):** Early readers connected this warning with later revelation about civil conflict, especially D&C 87 (December 25, 1832), which foretold war beginning with the rebellion of South Carolina.
- **"A desolating sickness" (v. 31):** Some early Saints associated this with the **cholera** pandemic that reached North America in 1832. The text itself places it among the signs of the last days generally.
- **"Keep these things from going abroad" (v. 72):** The revelation was first meant for Church members. It was later published in the 1833 Book of Commandments and in later editions of the Doctrine and Covenants.

## Why It Matters

D&C 45 is the Restoration's most sustained treatment of the **signs of the Second Coming** before D&C 88 and Joseph Smith—Matthew. It links the ancient promises (Enoch's Zion, the restoration of Israel, the Olivet prophecies) to the young Church's own tasks: translating scripture, gathering, and building Zion. It also opens with one of the most personal portraits of Christ as **advocate** in scripture (vv. 3–5).
